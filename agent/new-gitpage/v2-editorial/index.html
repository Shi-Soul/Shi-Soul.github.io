The long arc

I want robots that can do anything a person asks of them. That is a long way off, so I work on the part that seems most stubborn to me: a body that can actually move. Not a scripted walker, but a humanoid that controls all of its joints at once, under real physics, and learns that control by reinforcement.

The work has gone in stages. First, staying upright where there is almost no room for error, walking a beam no wider than a foot1, and keeping a policy improving after it leaves the simulator2. Then moving like a person, through fast and acrobatic human motion such as kung fu and dance3. Then many skills in one controller instead of one per policy4, and finally language as the interface: you type, it moves5.

Each step gives the robot more ways to move and a more natural way for people to ask. I think general-purpose robots will come from this kind of stacking, with good priors from human motion and a body that keeps learning, more than from any single big model.

1Narrow-terrain locomotion, IROS 2025.

2LoopSR, IROS 2025.

3KungfuBot, NeurIPS 2025.

4KungfuBot2, under review.

5TextOp, under review.
